2026 Late-Round Fantasy Football Value Analysis

Information cutoff: September 5, 2026
Format: 12-team, one-QB, half-PPR redraft with four-point passing touchdowns.

Decision summary

The five strongest risk-adjusted late-round targets are:

  1. Juwan Johnson, TE, NO — starting-TE production at a defense/kicker-round price.
  2. Dylan Sampson, RB, CLE — receiving efficiency plus a clearer No. 2 role and an injured starter ahead of him.
  3. Emmett Johnson, RB, KC — confirmed handcuff in a strong offense behind an injury-prone starter.
  4. Woody Marks, RB, HOU — standalone committee work plus contingent upside; David Montgomery is being priced near his ceiling.
  5. Brenton Strange, TE, JAC — ascending role, new contract and direct endorsement from the September 3 NFL Daily breakout episode.

Best late wide-receiver swing: Tre Tucker.
Best pure handcuff: Kaelon Black.
Best veteran floor after pick 175: Jerry Jeudy.
Best player to take around Round 8 rather than truly late: Michael Wilson.

The biggest current traps are Josh Jacobs at any normal redraft price, Jordyn Tyson without a usable IR slot, TreVeyon Henderson around pick 62, Makai Lemon around pick 94, Matthew Golden or De’Zhaun Stribling around pick 98, Zach Charbonnet around pick 153, and Sam LaPorta around pick 80.

How the board was built

  • FantasyPros half-PPR consensus, updated September 5 with 133 experts.
  • FFToday half-PPR ADP, updated September 3 from Underdog and Yahoo drafts.
  • PFF’s September 5 late-RB study.
  • DraftSharks’ preseason usage analysis, emphasizing starter snaps, routes and depth-chart order rather than raw exhibition totals.
  • September 5 injury and role reporting, including NFL.com’s daily roundup, PFN’s final sleeper analysis and CBS Sports’ model-based risk report.
  • Podscripts transcripts from NFL Daily with Gregg Rosenthal: August 21 fantasy trends, August 28 QB/TE strategy and late fliers, and September 3 “Making the Leap”.

ADP warning: prices vary sharply by platform. “Target” means take the player if he reaches that overall-pick window. “Do not take before” is a hard price ceiling, not a prediction that the player will last that long.

Strong values that are not truly late

Michael Wilson, WR, ARI — target around picks 80–95

Current ECR: 88; market ADP: 88.5.

NFL Daily opened its August 28 fantasy episode by asking why Wilson was still a late pick. The transcript notes that he finished WR10 in total scoring in 2025, and the discussion argued that both his route/catch-point talent and opportunity support the production. One speaker said he would consider Wilson in the fifth or sixth; Round 8 is still a strong price. This is not a hidden sleeper anymore, but it is one of the better ways to buy proven production after the early WR tiers.

Travis Kelce, TE, KC — target around picks 110–130

Current ECR: 104; market ADP: 123. Kelce finished near the top of the 2025 TE scoring table despite no longer being the overwhelming positional advantage of his prime. The discount compensates for age and Patrick Mahomes’ knee recovery. Juwan Johnson remains the superior pure price play because he costs another one to three rounds less.

Jake Ferguson, TE, DAL — target around picks 130–150

Current ECR: 125; market ADP: 142.5. Ferguson produced TE1-level aggregate scoring in 2025 and is a stable fallback if the premium and middle TE tiers disappear. Less upside than Strange, but a stronger established role.

Kyle Monangai, RB, CHI — only after pick 110

Current ECR: 111; market ADP: 111.5. He demonstrated the ceiling with 26 carries for 176 yards and 198 total yards when D’Andre Swift missed Week 9 last season. The problem is immediate availability: Monangai hyperextended his right knee on August 16 and remained week-to-week on September 1. Chicago kept him off IR, implying a return within the first four weeks. Draft him only if the bench can absorb an unavailable handcuff.

NFL Daily transcript weighting

The podcast evidence changes the board, but price and newer news override August opinions.

Calls strengthened by September developments

  • MarShawn Lloyd: NFL Daily anticipated a major opportunity if Josh Jacobs missed time. Jacobs was then placed on the exempt list on August 30. Lloyd’s thesis is stronger now.
  • Rhamondre Stevenson over TreVeyon Henderson: The August 21 episode explicitly argued that Stevenson remained New England’s lead back and should be drafted ahead of Henderson. Henderson then failed to practice after August 24 and remained out on September 5. Stevenson is the correct side at comparable prices.
  • Kaelon Black and Mike Washington: Both were Gregg late-round selections on August 28. Later depth-chart and preseason evidence supports them as the direct McCaffrey and Jeanty handcuffs.
  • Woody Marks: Gregg’s late pick gains support from the September 5 CBS model fading David Montgomery because Marks already produced more than 900 scrimmage yards as a rookie.
  • Brenton Strange and Tre Tucker: These were September 3 “Making the Leap” picks, making them the freshest NFL Daily player evaluations in this analysis.
  • A.J. Barner: Trevor Sikkema’s deep-TE case fits Seattle’s new coordinator and remains almost free.

Good player calls whose price has disappeared

  • Matthew Golden: NFL Daily loved him when Yahoo placed him around pick 127. The current blended market ADP is approximately 98 while his current consensus rank is 124. The player thesis can be right while the pick is bad. Do not chase before roughly 110–115.
  • De’Zhaun Stribling: NFL Daily discussed him as a Round 12 value. His current blended ADP has climbed to 98.5, ahead of his 117 ECR. Seven catches for 63 yards against Tennessee’s first-team defense and second-round capital are real positives, but veteran competition means he is no longer a value near pick 100. Target 125 or later.
  • Josh Downs: Gregg liked him in Rounds 10–11 for volume. Current blended ADP is 85.5, approximately Round 8. Buy only if the room lets him fall past 100.
  • Jaylen Warren: NFL Daily saw value when he lasted into Rounds 9–11. Current ADP is 68.5. At that price he is fairly valued, not a late steal—especially in half-PPR rather than full PPR.

Podcast strategy that survives the cross-check

  • Do not force Josh Allen or Lamar Jackson if the opportunity cost is an elite RB/WR. One-QB leagues still offer Goff, Love, Murray, Stafford and Baker late.
  • Premium TEs can create a large weekly positional edge, but the late pool is strong enough to wait: Johnson, Strange, Likely, Ferguson and Barner.
  • Favor late backs whose value changes dramatically with one depth-chart event. Corum, Black, Johnson, Mitchell and Washington satisfy that requirement.
  • Favor opportunity plus actual player quality. Michael Wilson is safer than a pure depth-chart lottery ticket; the handcuffs provide more ceiling than low-volume veteran WRs.

Yellow light — ceiling justifies the risk, but build insurance

  • Breece Hall: groin injury creates Week 1 uncertainty. Buy around his 34 ECR rather than his earlier preseason price.

Rounds 8–10

Priorities: Michael Wilson, MarShawn Lloyd, Blake Corum, then a falling Travis Kelce, Isaiah Likely or late QB.

  • Wilson supplies proven weekly WR production.
  • Lloyd and Corum provide the strongest combination of plausible early work and injury-away ceiling.
  • If quarterbacks have not been pushed up, Goff, Stafford, Jordan Love, Kyler Murray and Baker Mayfield let you continue drafting RB/WR.

Rounds 11–12

Priorities: Woody Marks, KC Concepcion at the right price, Mike Washington, Juwan Johnson, Keaton Mitchell.

  • Marks can produce without an injury and gains heavily if Montgomery misses time.
  • Concepcion is more attractive than Lemon at similar prices because Cleveland can manufacture five or six touches, but QB quality limits his ceiling.
  • Do not take Stribling or Golden simply because NFL Daily liked them at earlier, cheaper ADPs.

Rounds 13–14

Priorities: Juwan Johnson, Brenton Strange, Tre Tucker, Dylan Sampson, Emmett Johnson.

This is the highest-value area of the draft. Ideally leave it with at least two RBs and one TE/WR from this group.

Rounds 15–16

Priorities: Kaelon Black, A.J. Barner, Jerry Jeudy, Malik Davis, then defense and kicker only when required.

  • Black has league-changing contingent value if McCaffrey misses time.
  • Barner is a deep TE2; do not roster two TEs in shallow-bench formats unless the starter is risky.
  • Jeudy is the stable floor pick; Davis is the higher-variance RB ticket.

Recommended late-round portfolio

For six bench selections, do not draft six players with the same failure mode. A balanced construction:

  1. One immediate-use receiver: Michael Wilson if he falls; otherwise Tre Tucker or Jerry Jeudy.
  2. One late starting TE: Juwan Johnson or Brenton Strange.
  3. Two direct handcuffs: Emmett Johnson plus Kaelon Black, Keaton Mitchell or Blake Corum.
  4. One standalone/contingent RB: Woody Marks or Dylan Sampson.
  5. One asymmetric rookie/role swing: Mike Washington, KC Concepcion or De’Zhaun Stribling only at the correct price.

If you drafted McCaffrey, prioritize Black. If you drafted Kyren Williams, prioritize Corum. If you drafted Kenneth Walker, prioritize Emmett Johnson. Do not automatically handcuff every starter; take the backup only when the replacement role would be concentrated and valuable.
